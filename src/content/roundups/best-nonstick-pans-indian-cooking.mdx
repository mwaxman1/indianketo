## Why the Right Pan Matters for Indian Keto Cooking

Indian cooking is hard on pans. The high-heat tadka that blooms mustard seeds and cumin in smoking ghee. The slow-simmered curries that reduce for 45 minutes. The stovetop-to-broiler paneer tikka that needs a pan that can handle both. And for keto cooks, there's an added challenge: without the buffer of starchy rice or roti, any sticking means lost food — and lost food means lost macros.

A good nonstick pan for Indian keto cooking needs to do three things well: handle high heat without degrading, release food cleanly without excess oil, and survive the daily abuse of Indian cooking techniques. ### 2. Lodge 10.25-Inch Cast Iron Skillet

**Best for: High-heat Indian techniques and traditional cooking**

No pan handles heat like cast iron, and Indian cooking demands heat. When you're making a proper tadka — mustard seeds, cumin, dried red chilies, and curry leaves hitting smoking-hot ghee — cast iron is the traditional choice for a reason. It holds heat so well that the oil stays at temperature even after you add room-temperature spices, ensuring that essential 5-10 second bloom that releases aromatic oils.

For keto cooking specifically, cast iron has a hidden advantage: it adds trace amounts of iron to your food. Many people starting keto experience temporary iron fluctuations, and cooking acidic foods (like tomato-based curries) in cast iron can help. Just don't simmer highly acidic dishes for more than 30 minutes, or you'll strip the seasoning.

The Lodge comes pre-seasoned and ready to use. After each cook, rinse with hot water (no soap), dry thoroughly on the stove, and rub with a thin layer of oil. It sounds like work, but it becomes second nature — and the pan will outlast you.

**Best for:** High-heat tadka, charring keto roti, paneer tikka (stovetop to oven), blackening spices.
